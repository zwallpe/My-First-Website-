The Fantasy Football Lifecycle

You are acting as the General Manager (GM) and Head Coach of your custom NFL team. Here is how a full season flows:

1

The League Draft

Before the NFL season begins, you and your league mates draft real NFL players (QBs, RBs, WRs, TEs, K, DEF). Most leagues use a "Snake Draft" where picking order reverses each round (e.g., Pick 1 selects 1st in Rd 1, but 12th in Rd 2).

2

Weekly Head-to-Head Matchups

Every week during the NFL regular season, you set a starting lineup. As your real-life players accumulate yards, touchdowns, and catches on Sunday, they earn fantasy points for your team. The fantasy team with the most points wins that week's matchup!

3

Waiver Wire & Free Agency

Did a star player get injured, or did an unknown backup suddenly explode for 3 touchdowns? You can pick up available unclaimed NFL players off the "Waiver Wire" each week to improve your roster.

4

Trades & Lineup Decisions

Propose trades with other team managers to swap bench depth for top-tier starters. Swap out players who are on a "Bye Week" (when their real team isn't playing) or struggling with tough defensive matchups.

5

The Playoffs & Championship

Around NFL Week 15-17, the top teams in your league advance to a bracket-style tournament. Win out to take home the trophy, bragging rights, and the league pot!

Draft Strategy & Position Breakdown

Understanding the roster roles and draft value priorities for beginners.

QB • Quarterback

The Field General

  • Start: 1 in standard leagues
  • Scoring: Earns points through passing yards (25 yd = 1 pt) & passing TDs (4 pts).
  • Draft Tip: Dual-threat QBs who run (like Josh Allen or Lamar Jackson) are cheat codes because rushing yards are worth 2.5x more than passing yards!
  • Beginner Priority: Wait until middle rounds (Rounds 5-8).
RB • Running Back

The Workhorse

  • Start: 2 per week
  • Scoring: Earns points from rushing attempts, yards, and touchdowns.
  • Draft Tip: High-volume RBs are scarce due to NFL injuries. RBs that also catch passes out of the backfield have immense PPR value.
  • Beginner Priority: Very High! Draft 2-3 RBs in your first 4 rounds.
WR • Wide Receiver

The Playmakers

  • Start: 2 to 3 per week
  • Scoring: Driven by receptions, receiving yards, and receiving touchdowns.
  • Draft Tip: Target "WR1s" who are the main focal point of their NFL passing offense. High target volume leads to consistent points.
  • Beginner Priority: Very High! Balance your early picks between RBs and top WRs.
TE • Tight End

The Mismatch

  • Start: 1 per week
  • Scoring: Same as WRs (Catching yards/TDs).
  • Draft Tip: Only a few elite TEs score like top wide receivers (e.g., Travis Kelce, George Kittle). If you miss them, wait until late in the draft.
  • Beginner Priority: Medium/Low (unless drafting an elite top 3 TE).
FLEX • Flexible Slot

The Utility Player

  • Start: 1 or 2 per week
  • Eligibility: Usually RB, WR, or TE.
  • Draft Tip: ALWAYS put a player with a late-afternoon or Sunday/Monday night game in your Flex slot to keep roster flexibility if someone gets hurt late!
  • Beginner Priority: N/A (Filled by your bench depth).
K / DST • Kicker & Defense

Special Teams

  • Start: 1 Kicker, 1 Team Defense
  • Scoring: K (Field goals 3-5 pts), DST (Sacks, interceptions, low points allowed).
  • Draft Tip: NEVER draft a Kicker or Defense early!
  • Beginner Priority: Draft them in your final 2 rounds of the draft.

Fantasy Football Glossary

Search common terms, slang, and acronyms used by fantasy players.

PPR (Point Per Reception)

A scoring format where players earn 1 full point for simply catching a pass, regardless of yardage gained.

Handcuff

Drafting or holding the backup running back to your star starter as insurance in case the starter gets injured.

Sleeper

A player who is undervalued or flys under the radar during drafts, but has high upside potential to breakout.

Bust

A player drafted high in early rounds who drastically underperforms due to poor play, bad team offense, or injuries.

Waiver Wire

The pool of unowned free agent players. Claims are usually processed mid-week based on priority or FAAB bidding.

FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget)

A bidding system where every team gets a seasonal virtual dollar budget (e.g., $100) to bid blind on waiver players.

Stream / Streaming

Targeting players (especially QBs, Kickers, and Defenses) on a week-to-week basis based solely on good matchups rather than holding one player all season.

Target Share

The percentage of a team's total passing attempts that are thrown to a specific receiver.

Floor vs Ceiling

Floor: The minimum predictable points a player will score. Ceiling: The maximum potential points if everything goes right.
